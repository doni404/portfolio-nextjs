Two products can use a similar model and still create very different risks. A playful writing assistant is not the same kind of decision system as a tool involved in hiring.

## The historical milestone

The EU AI Act entered into force on August 1, 2024. The European Commission described a risk-based framework, with requirements depending on the kind of AI system and use. Entering into force did not mean every obligation applied immediately. [The Commission's 2024 announcement](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/ai-act-enters-force-2024-08-01_en)

This retrospective explains that product-design idea. It is not legal advice or a statement of the current compliance timetable; consult current official guidance and qualified advice for a real deployment.

## My product lens: start with the decision

Before discussing a model's benchmark, I would map what the product can do to a person.

Consider two illustrative tools. One suggests a better heading for a blog post. Another produces a score that an employer might use to reject an applicant. Even if their underlying technology overlaps, the consequences and review needs do not.

That is a useful design conversation regardless of the jurisdiction. It replaces a vague "Is our AI safe?" with concrete questions.

### Questions I would bring to a planning meeting

- Who is affected by the output, including people who never chose to use the app?
- Is the result a suggestion, a score, or an action?
- Can someone understand and challenge a consequential result?
- Who can override it, and do they have enough information to do so?
- What records would help investigate a complaint?

This is an original planning checklist, not a legal classification test or a complete compliance program.

## Transparency that is actually useful

My preference is a disclosure that tells the reader what happened: AI drafted this summary, the editor reviewed it, and the original sources are linked. A generic sparkle icon says much less.

The larger takeaway is that governance belongs in the product brief, not only in a document written after launch. Start with the people, decisions, and possible consequences. Then choose how much automation makes sense.
